# Write the strategy note

Write a player-facing Markdown primer grounded only in the sealed job's aggregate and query evidence. Aim for 800–1,400 words; the publisher accepts a 600–1,500-word safety range.

Use the language requested by the user. When none is stated, use the invocation prompt's language rather than the embedded manual note's language. Keep the required headings below exact even when the body uses another language.

Use these headings exactly and in this order.

## Title

Start with `# <Deck title>`.

## Theme & Story

Frame the deck's flavor/story in 2–4 evocative sentences. Ground it in commanders, creature types, card-name motifs, mechanics, and any manual note. Do not invent lore or claim unsupported narrative intent.

## Deck Snapshot

Give a compact statistical picture:

- deck size, lands, average mana value, and expected lands in the opening hand;
- meaningful mana-curve concentrations or pressure points;
- dominant types and subtypes;
- strongest broad buckets and strategically defining granular buckets.

Interpret the numbers instead of dumping the aggregate.

## Mechanical Identity

State the primary mechanical plan and up to three secondary mechanical themes. Explain how the active commander configuration changes card roles or rewards the deck's repeated actions.

## Game Plan

Describe the principal engine, credible secondary plan, pivot conditions, and how accumulated advantage becomes a win. Distinguish actual closing routes from generic value generation.

## Key Cards

Identify roughly 6–12 deck-specific cards. For each, state its role and why it is central, irreplaceable, connective, protective, or decisive. Do not fill the list with unrelated staples.

## Synergies & Engines

Name both sides of each important relationship and the granular roles they join. Explain the trigger or resource flow precisely. Prefer a few high-signal engines over a catalogue of superficial pairs.

## Combos & Loops

For every verified loop, state:

1. prerequisites and starting zones;
2. ordered actions and costs;
3. the repeated state and net result;
4. stopping conditions and interruption points.

Place powerful non-loop interactions under **Synergies & Engines**. Write “No deterministic loop was verified from the available evidence” when appropriate.

## How to Pilot

Use all four required subsections.

### Opening Hand & Mulligans

Describe deck-specific mana, setup, interaction, and engine requirements. Explain which attractive-looking hands should be rejected.

### Early Game

Explain development order, setup priorities, and when the commander should wait.

### Midgame

Explain engine sequencing, resource conversion, interaction timing, and plan pivots.

### Late Game

Explain closing routes, inevitability, recovery decisions, and how to avoid stalling with value but no finish.

## What to Protect

Identify the commander, engine, bridge, payoff, or bottleneck that deserves protection. State what can be allowed to die and what cannot.

## Weaknesses & Recovery

Name structural weaknesses supported by the curve, bucket density, reliance on zones/permanent types, or narrow engine pieces. Give realistic recovery and pivot lines already present in the deck.

## Alternate Commanders

Treat all active partners jointly throughout the note. For each flagged alternate, add `### If <name> commands` and explain changes to priorities, card roles, curve, sequencing, protection, and win routes. When no alternate is configured, state that plainly.

## Grounding rules

- Name supporting cards for every important mechanical claim.
- Use natural prose rather than formal citations.
- Preserve the user's strategic intent when it agrees with card facts.
- State material uncertainty briefly; omit speculative filler.
- Offer no upgrade suggestions or cards outside the deck.
